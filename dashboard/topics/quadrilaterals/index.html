Lab 3 · Mid-point theorem

Join the mid-points — what happens?

Drag A, B, C. \(D\) and \(E\) are mid-points of \(AB\) and \(AC\). The mid-point theorem says \(DE \parallel BC\) and \(DE = \dfrac{1}{2}BC\).

Try it

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(D\) and \(E\) are mid-points of \(AB\) and \(AC\). If \(BC = 12\), what is \(DE\)?

Use mid-pt. thm.: \(DE = \dfrac{1}{2}BC\).

Lab 4 · Intercept theorem

Equal intercepts on transversals

Three parallel lines cut equal intercepts on one transversal. Drag the gold handles — intercepts on the other transversal stay equal. Reason: intercept thm.

Proportional form (inside a triangle)

A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally. Drag \(D\) on \(AB\); \(E\) moves so \(DE \parallel BC\).
