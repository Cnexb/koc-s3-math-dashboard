JM28 · Lesson 28

Special Line and Centres in Triangles

Four special lines — altitude, median, angle bisector and perpendicular bisector — meet at the orthocentre, centroid, in-centre and circumcentre.

Part 1 · Special lines & centres

1. Line → centre explorer

Pick a special line. Its three lines concur at the matching centre — \(H\), \(G\), \(I\) or \(O\). Try the triangle presets (acute / right / obtuse / isosceles / equilateral).

Altitudes meet at the orthocentre \(H\).

Part 2 · Properties (worksheet A–D)

2. Angle bisector & perpendicular bisector

An angle bisector splits the angle into two equal parts. A perpendicular bisector is perpendicular to a side and passes through its mid-point — any point on it is equidistant from the end-points.

If \(AD\) bisects \(\angle BAC\), then \(\angle BAD = \angle CAD\).

Part 3 · Quick checks

3. Centroid ratio

On median \(AD\), the centroid \(G\) divides \(AG : GD = 2 : 1\). If \(AG = 10\) cm, find \(GD\).

Use the \(2:1\) ratio from vertex to mid-point.

4. Which centres may lie outside?

(Ch 28 E Q19) In an obtuse-angled triangle, which centres can lie outside the triangle?

In-centre and centroid always lie inside.
