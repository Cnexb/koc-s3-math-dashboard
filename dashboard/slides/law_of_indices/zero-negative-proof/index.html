Law 3 · Proof

Zero Exponent

Why is any non-zero number to the power of 0 equal to 1?

\[ a^0 = 1 \quad (a \neq 0) \]

Consider dividing equal powers using the Quotient Law \((m=k, n=k)\):

\[ \dfrac{a^k}{a^k} = a^{k - k} = a^0 \]

But any non-zero number divided by itself equals 1:

\[ \dfrac{a^k}{a^k} = 1 \]
Therefore:   \(a^0 = 1 \quad (a \neq 0)\)
Law 4 · Proof

Negative Exponents

What does a negative exponent really mean?

\[ a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n} \quad (a \neq 0) \]

Write \(-n\) as \(0 - n\) and apply the Quotient Law:

\[ a^{-n} = a^{0 - n} = \dfrac{a^0}{a^n} \]

Substitute \(a^0 = 1\):

\[ \dfrac{a^0}{a^n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n} \]
In general:   \(a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n} \quad\text{and}\quad \left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)^n\)
Special Case

Why is \(0^0\) Undefined?

Notice the strict condition \(a \neq 0\) in \(a^0 = 1\):

\[ 0^0 = 0^{k - k} = \dfrac{0^k}{0^k} = \dfrac{0}{0} \]

Division by zero is mathematically undefined:

\(0^0\) is undefined!
