Law 5 · Proof

Power of a Power

Why do we multiply indices when raising a power to a power?

\[ (a^m)^n = a^{mn} \]

For example, \((a^3)^2\) means 2 groups of \(a^3\):

\[ (a^3)^2 = a^3 \times a^3 = a^{3+3} = a^{3 \times 2} = a^6 \]

In general, \(n\) groups of \(m\) factors:

\[ (a^m)^n = \underbrace{a^m \times a^m \times \cdots \times a^m}_{n\text{ factors}} = a^{\overbrace{m + m + \cdots + m}^{n\text{ times}}} = a^{mn} \]
In general:   \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)
Law 6 · Proof

Fractional Indices

What number does \(a^{\frac{1}{2}}\) represent?

Square it using the Power of a Power rule:

\[ \left(a^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^2 = a^{\frac{1}{2} \times 2} = a^1 = a \]

Since squaring \(a^{\frac{1}{2}}\) produces \(a\), by definition:

\[ a^{\frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{a} \]
In general:   \(a^{\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a} \quad\text{and}\quad a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m\)
Complete Summary

All 6 Laws of Indices

1. Product:
\(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\)
2. Quotient:
\(a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\)
3. Power of Power:
\((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)
4. Zero Index:
\(a^0 = 1 \quad (a \neq 0)\)
5. Negative Index:
\(a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}\)
6. Fractional Index:
\(a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}\)
